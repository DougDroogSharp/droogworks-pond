# Hel's Bet
*Book 2 of The Hel's Bet Series · Doug Sharp · 2015*

> "Work for the Singularity to max your odds of living long. Don't bother if you've got a taste for dirt."
>
> — Heloise Chin

---

A burst of lead claws the tiles on the cockpit roof. A slug rips a screeching groove in the thick windshield.

“PUNCH IT!” I shout.

BOOMGROWL! Energia’s engines roar to life. Twenty howling thrusters jam the seat hard into my spine.

“Whee doggies!” I whoop. Back into the wild black yonder.

Space shuttle Enterprise scrams on treadmarks of smoke up, up, up into the grey Kazakh sky.

Energia growls and Enterprise shudders. We’re pulling some real g’s and straining to breathe. Launch acceleration always makes me feel like a bear is squatting on my chest.

I roll my heavy head to look at Heloise Chin: demon-child geek goddess and instigator of this mad shuttle launch. Hel gives me back the sweetest, clearest smile. Her green, green eyes radiate joy. My heart does a cartwheel—I didn’t know Heloise Chin could smile like that.

Headphones bark gunfire from Baikonur Control Center. Someone wails in Russian and then another burst of machinegun fire clatters through my headset. WTF?

“You ok, Vasili?”

Damned Mafiya goons are shooting at our friend Vasili Kropotkin!

“Vasili? Answer me!” I visualize Kropotkin riddled with bullets as he punched the launch button to save our asses.

Vasili is our corrupt Kazakh space official buddy who helped us steal this mighty Russian Energia booster to launch space shuttle Enterprise. About 90 seconds ago he put his ass on the line for us when he punched the launch button while under fire.

I look at Hel. Her smile fades as she waits for an answer from Vasili. We hear only the bellow of Energia’s mighty thrusters. Their roaring vibrations jangle right up my spine. There’s nothing we can do but hope the good guys beat the Mafiya down at Baikonur Cosmodrome. Hard to be elated when friends are on the line.

Tension and g-force make for deep, deliberate breaths.

My headphone crackles with Vasili Kropotkin’s distinctive growl, “Enterprise? You are OK? Enterprise, answer!”

“WOOHOO! Good to hear your voice, Vasili.”

Hel smiles one of her rare and radiant smiles. “You OK?”

“I am surwivor so wery A-OK. Mafiya shits haf run.”

What just happened? Um… launched a shuttle from Baikonur in a hail of bullets with a gunfight raging in the control room! I put on my professional comm voice. “Some idiot shot the shuttle. I got bullet holes in the tiles forward.”

Kropotkin, “I saw on wideo the shooting at you. How is pressure?”

Hel, “Nominal.” No punctures!

The combination of getting shot at, relief at Kropotkin’s survival, my usual launch nerves, Hel’s sweet smile, and the unfamiliar thunder of the Energia’s twenty thrusters overload my circuits.

I picture the bastard who peppered Enterprise with machinegun bullets. “It was Daddy Delp, right, Vasili? That Chechen Mafiya bastard Tommy-gunned my shuttle!”

“Da. Much happens quickly but you are safe. Flight looking good. No damage is done. Is begin pitch-ofer sequence.”

Nothing for me to do but ride the flame. Energia/Enterprise churns my guts as it rolls to a new angle driving for orbit. The bear hunkers down on my lungs, making breathing wheezy—heavier than any launch I’ve done. Energia is one mighty, mighty booster.

As we accelerate, air heats Enterprise’s exterior and hot, plasticky smells tingle my fear circuits. All we need is one hole to melt through our cheap tile skin to shorten our trip catastrophically. Can’t dwell on our danger—nothing we can do about it.

“Are you safe down there?”

“All is A-OK here. I haf gun after Blegnor drop it. Blegnor and Gogol did running away. And Boss Delp is… How I can say? Gogol send Boss Delp to launch pad in car with crazy dancing doctor woman.”

“Say again? Gogol what? His nurse did what?”

“Gogol send crazy woman drive…”

I ask, “When did Gogol show up? Start at the beginning.”

“Gogol and Nurse Motorcade come into control center with Boss Delp and son, yelling loud about Channel Zilch persons playing trick. Blegnor point Tommy gun and Gogol say stop countdown.”

“I remember that real well. Real well.” My nerves are a bundle of thrashing worms. Calm, Oolfson.

Vasili, “Ah. Here is come doctor of shuttles, Greetch. Welcome, friend. Launch is good, no?”

Gritch’s scratchy voice comes through loud and clear. “Good work, y’all. WOOWEE that Energia rides a mean cloud of fire and smoke! Had me a good gander at the launch outdoors. Ain’t never missed watchin a shuttle launch, wasn’t bout to start. I just seen a bunch of them damn Mafiya scramming so I ducked until their butts was dwindlin.”

I say, “Great to hear from you, old buddy. Thanks to your work prepping the shuttle, looks like we’ll survive the launch.”

“Fry my nuts if it don’t look like it, ya lucky lunatics.”

Vasili, “All systems go.”

The shuttle shakes. I hear a rattle and then a pop. Sounds like it came from the port side—not a relaxing sound. Over the headphone comes Vasilis’s voice. “Max Q*. Systems all are go.”

[*Altitude at which atmospheric drag peaks, about 30 kilometers up when the speed’s high and where the air’s thin -“Max Q is a Houston-based rock band whose members are all NASA Astronauts.” —Wikipedia.org]

I ask, “Vas, you getting any red lights? We just had a severe vibration and a loud bang from port. You locate that, Hel?”

Heloise Chin looks worried! “A metallic megapop on the port. Anything show up down there?”

“Is looking wery good on control panel. Is wibrating now?”

“Shit, shit, shit!” The shuttle/Energia starts to slowly rotate around the vertical axis.

Hel, “We’re starting to spin.”

“Energia’s rolling clockwise around vertical axis.” My gut feels the rate of spin increase. Through the windshield, the stars and horizon start to rotate. I close my eyes. It was fun while it lasted. Lots worse ways to snuff it.

Hel, “What the fuck should we do, Gritch?”

Long seconds of silence before Gritch’s voice comes through steady and strong. “Ya got one shot at stabilizing. Jam at rudder hard. Remember that cut-out switch we…”

“Got it.” My piloting nerves go icy-calm. I grunt as I raise my ten-ton arm to flip the red switch Gritch installed to cut out the control surfaces. I jam the right rudder pedal hard. Nothing. “No-go!” We continue to spin. My gut melts and starts to churn with fear.

“Give er a sec. Keep stompin at pedal. Air’s mighty thin up there.”

Hel, “Spin slowing.” We exchange blank looks. Too soon to smile.

“Got it!” I see it and feel it in my gut. The world outside slowly slows its rotation and then slides to a stops I let out a heavy lungful. We gradually start to roll in the other direction so I let off the right rudder pedal, tweak it a tad—the stars and horizon steady.

Time to smile. I turn to Hel. Her bright green eyes beam at me.

I shout, “YEEHA! Thanks a bunch, Gritch. Got that spin under control. Makes this astronaut feel useful!”

“Fuckin hotshot. Thank your sweet ass I was here to dig you out of the pig shit.”

“Thanks, you sweet ass.”

Gritch chuckles. “That Energia’s a champ. Them rocket gimbals was workin overtime to keep y’all from tumblin and burnin. Handin back to Vasili.”

“Talk later, old buddy.”

“Y’all have fun messin up that beaut of a shuttle. Glad I won’t have to hose it down.”

“We’ll keep it clean.”

“Heard that un before.”

Vasili, “Is miracle. I wish you work for me, Greetch.” His voice steadies and he’s all business again. He’s never capcommed a launch before and is eating this up. “Is in fifteen second booster separation. Loud noise will come.”

I’m surprised, haven’t kept track of minutes. “Roger that, Baikonur.”

“Is now.”

KACHUNKKKK! Explosive bolts blow off the four side booster blocks and we’re down to the huge four central nozzles. The bear on my chest loses some weight as our acceleration drops. I flash back to the scary solid boosters that powered the shuttles from the Cape: Challenger.

The hot plastic smell in Enterprise’s cockpit takes on overtones of burning. I heap that warning sign onto my mental pile of can’t-do-a-thing-about-it.

“Enterprise? You are ok?” Evidently Vasili has some doubts about our Energia. Can’t blame him after that roll.

“Separation noise anxiety. No prob. So what happened in the control room with little Delp?”

Blegnor Delp is the pimple-assed son and heir of Mafiya Boss Hwadco Delp. Mafiya thugs made me “train” Baby Delp to be an astronaut in one week. Delp was slated to take my place as pilot on this launch until Gogol pulled a power move and double-crossed the Delps to put me back in Enterprise’s saddle.

Gogol’s got his own agenda: put Enterprise in geosynch orbit hauling two server farms loaded with all sorts of nasty digital STD’s. He figured his plan would work out better if I flew the shuttle rather than leaving it to the IQ-challenged gangster heir.

To encourage us to follow his plan, Gogol promised to kill Hel’s Dad, Manuel Chin, and my shuttle-fixing buddy, Gritch Neubart, if we screw up the server farm delivery. Gangster motivational techniques are effective.

I dump that thought onto my useless piles of fears.

“After mafiya shits come Control Room, Blegnor stand wery angry beside me with Tommy gun. Then on wideo monitor I see crazy nurse drive into launch area and Boss Delp get out of car. Nurse drive away fast and Boss aim Tommy gun at Enterprise and spray lead at you. I think it is better you launch even if I am shot so I yell and punch button.”

Vasili has guts. Never got to know him. “You did us a huge one. Boss Delp tried to bring down Enterprise with a Tommy gun and you stopped him. He's going to come after your hide.”

“No chance. Standing by Energia at launch wery unhealthy.”

I suck in a breath. “Ouch. He's toast.”

“Is smoke.”

Gritch butts in, “Bastard had it comin. Douchewad shootin at my shuttle.”

“Da. Little Delp see father burned on wideo he is crying and dropping Tommy gun. Gogol saying over and over to little Delp that evil Channel Zilch people kill your father. I grab gun and shoot into floor and Blegnor run screaming away like little boy.”

Hel, “What about that angstromcocked spam-banger Gogol?”

Vasili laughs. “Up Gogol gets from veelchair and runs fast away.”

Hel, “Didn’t need that wheelchair—cute! Gogol offed Boss Delp and now little Delp thinks he’s a hero! Gogol used our launch to hijack Delp’s gang.”

Vasili whistles. “Is make sense.”

I shake my head as it sinks in. “Doesn’t do us a lick of good. Gogol wants the server farm orbited and his threat to your Dad and Gritch still stands.”

Energia provides rumbling sound FX.

Hel, “We’ll break him.”

Gritch, “Hell of a story. Now you’re up there I’m gonna hit the road. Gonna hook up with Manuel and get the hell back home.”

Hel, “We owe you.”

“Damn straight. Buy me a case of rum if you make it back home.”

I say, “Thanks, pal. We’ll buy you an oil tanker full of Angostura Single Barrel Dark. Karabuk’ll give us a discount on the ship. Now hit the road.” Gonna miss that skinny runt.

Vasili, “Flight looking good. Are feeling more sounds or wibrations or spinink?”

Enterprise still groans like a beast but it’s not getting worse. No spin. “Nah.”

I return to happier topics. “A vaporized Papa Delp is swell news for you and the Kazakhs. Simplifies your escape after launch.”

“Is reports of all gangsters running away Baikonur. Gritch, Manuel, and I not needing fast get-away.”

“I guess it’s up to you to set Ishwald loose, with the Mafiya gone.”

My useless-worry-pile sheds a few tons: Ishwald is no longer a concern. Captain Tobias Ishwald: ex-SEAL, ex-Head of NASA Security who blames me because he washed out of astronaut training. We’ve tussled plenty. I’ve got two scars on my arms from where the bastard bit me.

Stealing Enterprise from under his nose got him fired and didn’t endear me to Toby. He’s a bullheaded cuss and I gotta give him credit for sneaking into Baikonur last night to save his stolen shuttle. Too bad the Mafiya caught him and stuck the claustrophobic S.O.B. into a morgue drawer. Too, too bad. I try to feel sympathy for Ishwald but it’s a no-go.

“I will send Kazakh to open Eeshvalt drawer after all responsible for launch have scrammed.”

“Be sure to warn your friend about Ishwald’s teeth.” Like I said, the bastard bit me twice.

Enterprise flies straight and true propelled by the GROWLLLING Energia. The plastic burn smell abates.

A tip from Mick Oolfson: Riding a rocket’s the best fun there is, even if the launch is iffy as heck.

I can tell Kropotkin loves capcomming our mission by the way he says, “Is T minus forty-fife second until separation.”

That lights my pilot’s heart. I look over to Hel and ask, “Ready to give it to me?”

Hel smirks, pulls back to a genuine smile, and nods.

Kropotkin, “Thirty second. Entering now China airspace.”

Hel and I exchange raised eyebrows. “Cool,” she mouths. Her eyes snap back to her laptop. My eyes linger on her profile.

“Fifteen second. Then Enterprise flying on your control.”

“Roger, Baikonur. Ready for final staging.” The thought of actually flying Enterprise sweeps aside my Everest of worry. Flying spaceships is what I live for.

“Ten. Parameters of systems normal, Enterprise.”

“Fife.”

The roar of the Energia has receded from the center stage of my awareness. Funny what you get used to, but you never get used to the g-force bear on your chest.

“Mark.” A BOOM and then silence.

Which I break with a heartfelt, “YEEHAA!”

Explosive charges sever the mount points that bound Enterprise to Energia. The bear jumps off my chest: weightlessness. Just a tantalizing taste of it before our Orbital Maneuvering System (OMS) kicks in. We’ll have to wait until orbit for real microgravity fun.

“We gif you wery good launch. Efery luck to Enterprise on her journey.”

“Thanks, Baikonur. Mighty sweet booster you guys rented us. That thing laid down some serious horsepower. Gave us Zilchonauts a kickass ride.”

Kropotkin sounds wistful. “I will miss fire and noise of beautiful launches. But I go happy to retirement.”

“Luck to you, Vasili, and to all our Kazakh friends down there on their retirements. Glad you don’t have Boss Delp on your ass.”

Vasili whispers, “Farewell, lovely Heloise Chin. I haf done my most best work for you.”

Hel, “Thanks, Vasili. You risked your ass for us. We owe you. Thank your team. Now scram. Head for the hills. Ollie-ollie-oxenfree.”

Kropotkin breathes into his mike, thinking, then says quietly, “Two more things I haf tell you. First is for Heloise only.”

“Okay.” What Vasili wants, he gets. I toggle off my comm link.

I look over at Hel as she gets the message and her face is startlingly legible for once. Her expression starts as anger, softens into confusion, and then giddiness signaled by a wide-eyed grin. I swear I’ll put a grin like that on her face one day.

Hel giggles, shakes her head and yells, “Everyone back on comm. The big lug left me a cool love offering in the shuttle bay. It’ll stay my little secret until we open it. I think a lot of you, too, Vasili, but not in that way.”

I click in and hear his resigned sigh. “Ride the gift with Vasili’s love, crazy American hacker woman with soul of infinite complication and beauty.”

Hel, “Thanks. True thanks.”

“One more thing I haf say to Mick.”

I say, “Shoot, Vas.”

“I am eating launch beans*.”

[*Eating beans cooked during launch prep’s a NASA custom.]

“Good on you, Vas. Now get.”

Hel gives me a look. “Beans?”

My turn to smirk. “It’s like pissing on a bus tire*.”

[*Gagarin had to take a piss before his historic flight so he pissed on the tire of the bus that brought him to the launch site. Every cosmonaut and astronaut launching from Baikonur follows that tradition. Woman piss in a jar and pour it.]

Her eyebrows clench and her mouth scrunches. Then she turns her head to me real slow. “Ready for ignition?”

Man, am I ready. This is the first time I actually fly this crate if you don’t count my genius spin recovery.

I command, “Light my fire.”

She snickers and turns to her laptop, her one true love. “Five, Four, Three, two, yes*, ignition.”

[*Hel speaks her own language based on ancient hacker jargon and her own geek wit: one=yes and zero=no.]

A muffled bass growl then a small kick that flattens to a fine, steady acceleration. Our first taste of weightlessness is over. G-forces are light—the bear on my chest is just a teddy.

Those AmeRocket OMS thrusters burn real sweet. Got a throatier sound than the ones on the stock shuttles, like a street rod rather than a muscle car. As they burn the scorched-plastic odor dissipates. One less ratchet of tension.

Hat damn! Piloting a shuttle after all those wasted years of ground-pounding. I’ve got my hand on the joystick and my attention on my control-panel-duct-taped laptop. Hel controls thrust and I aim the shuttle. She wrote a program that gives me a graphic display of the most efficient path to orbit and my deviation from it. It plays like a cheesy old computer game with epic 3D sound FX and full-body rumble. I give a gentle pull and watch my display track our excursion from optimal insertion path. Push and twist slightly and I’m back. Dead on.

Energia will shove us 98% to our target. Our OMS thrusters need to get us that last 2%. Accuracy is vital—our first move is to rendezvous with the glitched sat which is our ticket to geosynchronous orbit.

We team well. Hel and I’ve practiced this only about a dozen times, so it feels raw and new to me. I’m used to practicing crucial maneuvers umpteen hundred times for NASA jaunts.

Heloise and I keep up a steady chatter, coordinating amplitude of thrust with trajectory to build the perfect insertion vector. Thoughts of gangsters don’t clomp around in my brain. Time goes like it does when you’re doing what you’re meant to. Who knows how fast time passes? Heloise knows—to the millisec.

Hel, “Cutting thrust on mark plus five seconds. Mark.”

I don’t want it to end. I make a pouty face at Hel but she’s glued to her screen. Then I remember what’s about to happen. I relish my goofy grin.

“Mark. Four. Three. Two. Yes. Cut throttle.”

I flip the switch and the teddy bear jumps off of my chest.

Silence.

I love this moment. I live for this moment. Once you’ve tasted the first moment, you can’t wait for the next moment exactly like it, however long that moment takes to reach and however many schemes you need to scam to get to that always new, always sweet, first moment of weightlessness.

“YEEEEhaaaa! Ladies and gentleman, we are floating in space.”

I reach down, pop my restraining straps, and float free of my pilot’s seat. I’m usually a busy boy right about now on NASA trips, but Zilch is bare bones when it comes to scheduled tasks. Hel has a few tests scheduled at some undefined point in the near future but right now I’m self-scheduled to have me some fun. I’m gonna wing it like I never could before. No busy-work!

First things first. “How is pressure?”

“Nominal. We’re tight.”

I turn to yell at the middeck hatch. “Hey Darthy, Richard! You guys okay?”

Darthy Vader is our resident videographer, accordion player, and mother-figure. She’s a tie-dyed space hippy who’s been scheming with Hel for years to get into space. Darthy carries a little GoPro® video cam almost everywhere she goes, documenting our collective Zilch insanity. She’s and Hel have got a thing about the Singularity, the Geek Cargo Cult God. Darthy is a magnificent fiftyish woman with a penumbra of red hair and a laugh that sounds like fresh-baked bread. She’s my best friend onboard Enterprise. Unfortunately she’s got a major thing for our pill-popping Hyperstar Spacecaster, Richard Head.

How could I forget to mention my “pal” Richard? He’s a failed television talent who got conned into believing hosting a space shuttle reality show would be his big chance. Richard is one of the few beings who raises my hackles every time we meet. He’s got a hypnotic newsreader voice that bulls-eyes every button in my psyche—scares the bejabbers out of me. I tried hard to get him kicked off the mission, but his gangster fan club kept him on the payroll.

In answer to my query, Dar’s head pops through the interdeck hatch. Her eyes pour pure rapture, her mouth oohs in disbelieving awe. She takes long, quivery breaths and blinks her eyes. I look at her and shake my head in wonder. “You made it.”

“I made it. This old space hippy made it into space!”

“Where’s your video cam?”

“Damn. Guess filming’s got to wait. I got plenty of action footage Earthside. Now I’m in space. I need some me time.”

A pounding from middeck. Darthy ducks down to check it. Pops up again. “Head puked in his helmet.”

“Go!” She pops down through the hatch and I bounce right after her.

We save Richard from drowning in his own puke. Ah, the Romance of Outer Space. Living The Dream. The familiar smell of vomit.

Not exactly the video fodder we need for our Zilch show.

When Darthy and I release Head from his puke-filled Orlan spacesuit he’s not pleased to discover Mick Oolfson has taken his buddy Blegnor’s place. One of the prime reasons Dick Head underwhelms me is that head gangster Hwadco Delp was a big fan of his and Head buddied up to him.

I won’t render the dialog because the man is quite ill and it’s disgusting how he yells at me while he pukes. Yells at me while I chase down stinking, floating globules of his puke with a hand-vac, mind you. The boy’s got the zero-g gut-quakes bad. I have to empathize. SAS (Space Adaptation Sickness) always gets most of the rookies and some of the veterans during the first couple of hours in orbit. Space doesn’t get me, but the sea…

The sedative Darthy feeds him kicks in and he stops spewing and swearing. Dar and I snug Richard in a sleeping bag with plenty of restraints. We continue vacuuming his floating globes of puke. Greater love hath no astronaut than to suppress his gag reflex for his teammate.

Dar and I make eye contact as we’re chasing down the last floating spheres of spew, and crack ourselves up. Head snarls wetly at us from his bag.

I give Darthy a wry grin. “Welcome to space.”

She chuckles and hugs me.

I ask, “Ready for a peek?”

Darthy unhugs me and kicks off for the flightdeck hatch and almost makes it, bouncing off the ceiling about a foot off course. I push off and bulls-eye the hatch, turn and offer her a hand, but she waves me away. She gathers her legs, aims, springs, misses again, and rebounds.

“Take it easy. It’s okay to crawl along the walls until ya get the hang of flying.”

“Cram it, punk. This time for sure.”

I watch as she gives an itty-bitty push and spends twenty seconds drifting across the middeck. She windmills her arms futilely as soon as she sees she’s going to miss the hatch by a meter. She’ll learn.

“I’ll leave you to practice. I’m going to get me an eyeful.” I kick through the flightdeck hatch and drift toward Hel. “How goes?”

“Peachy. Scary-clean.”

“When will you need me?”

“About twenty minutes. You and Dar doing funtime?”

“We had that little matter of Head turned inside out to mop up. 3D Pukeman.”

“Wakka Wakka. Better hit the viewport or Dar’ll beat you to first peek.”

I pivot. Darthy drifts to the port grinning. She grabs hold of the sill and presses her nose to the glass—first of us to look back at Earth. I watch her watch. A moment for her.

She gasps. It’s not a good gasp.

“Shit.” She turns and her eyes are wide. Not a good wide.

I flip myself toward her and look out the port. Earth vast and blue. It looks right.

“’T’sup?”

“The wing. Look!”

I look and my mouth does a big, silent OH! The wing is burned—black charred foam melted off the tip exposing stark metal spars. A hand shoves me aside and Heloise looks out at the ruined wing. We take it in and turn to look each other eye to eye.

I say, “That explains the spin. Sucks to lose that wing.”

Hel, “Don’t need it.”

She’s correct but it’s just not right. The wings are ornamental for our one-way mission. “But it’s a shame.”

I take another look at the injured wing and feel a twinge of sorrow for Enterprise. Then the fact of our safe launch hits me and I grin a big one—She made it! Enterprise made it into space! After all the indignities and abuse she suffered, she beat the bastards.

Enterprise is the space shuttle prototype used to test everything from structural strength to ability to glide. After her tests NASA was supposed to upgrade her to join the shuttle fleet, but all the big and miniscule design changes resulting from her tests made it cheaper to build a new shuttle than to bring her up to code. Since her last glide to Earth from a 747 she’s been a PR showpiece, which made it easier to heist her from Dulles when she was on her way to the shuttle reunion at The Cape.

I pat her on the sill of her porthole. Well done, old girl. You beat the NASA bastards and made it to the Big Cosmic Ball. I’m proud I stole you. You’re a real spaceship now.

Dar butts into my precious moment. “Any danger?” She’s armed with her video cam, recording the burnt wing, our first vista of Earth, and my gut reaction to the harm done to my shuttle.

Hel, “Can’t tell about the starboard wing but I’m not reading any damage that’ll screw the flight.”

We’ve got no viewport on the starboard side, no way of assessing possible damage to that wing until I do an EVA.

I say, “Reminds us we’re stuck here until we complete our mission. Speaking of mission, did you reach your Dad?”

Hel smiles. Nice to know she’s still got a few vestigial shards of human feeling. “A Kazakh freed him from the room Delp locked him in and Kropotkin put him on comm when you two were chasing Head’s breakfast. We had a chat. He’s a happy man we got up here uncharred and that his investment is intact; you decide the ranking.

“He’s splitting Kazakh space camp. Going to spend some time in an undisclosed hidey-hole. The ground team is making an orderly exit from Baikonur. Pop last saw Vasili in a black leather jacket walking toward the shed where he parked his truckload of Monkees albums. Mafiya hightailed it when word of the Delp-fry spread. We talk again in five, eight days, thereabouts.”

“Any word of Ishwald?” I dislike ex-CaptainToby but he went way beyond the call of duty to track us down. Props to him. Brave lad except for his claustrophobia.

“Dad said he is still squealing in that morgue drawer. Pop will be long gone before a Kazakh janitor lets the Captain out.”

Dar’s back at the port. This time she smiles. Her video cam floats on a tether beside her, not recording Dar’s own emotional reaction to her first sight of Earth’s magnificence. “Earth’s a beautiful planet.”

Hel’s brows squinch together, thinking on that one. She looks down at her feet, kicks a precise trajectory from a bulkhead and drifts portside to join Dar. I’ll give them a few minutes to hog the view. They wrap arms around each other’s shoulders.

Earth is a beautiful planet indeed to create such wondrous creatures.

I feel joy—no other word for it. I MADE IT! I’m in space again! And I beat Ishwald. I start to push that thought away as unworthy, but dive in and wallow in it. ‘Fuck you, ground-pounder!’ I shout in my heart, feeling delightfully unclean. I did it again, the odds a thousand times worse than the first time I drove a shuttle. Becoming an astronaut is nothing compared to stealing a spaceship and surviving Heloise Chin.

Gosh, she’s gorgeous floating there. Hel turns her head and catches me looking at her. I don’t look away. She says bluntly, “You and me—flush your hopes.”

Darthy, with her nose pressed against the port window, pretends to ignore us.

Hel, “Enterprise is close quarters. We need to lower the temperature between us so I’ll stop leading you around by your willy. No more testosterone surfing. Let’s be pals.”

I say with my mouth, “Sure. Let’s be pals.”

But Hel’s eyes read what my eyes say: hope. In space Heloise will see me in my element. Mick Oolfson at the top of his game should be enough for any woman, even the heart-free Heloise Chin.

Hel says nothing and I can’t read her slight frown. Sadness for me, for herself, or annoyance at having a resident stalker? I break the awkward moment by trying humor. “Wouldn’t sexual tension between us boost Channel Zilch’s ratings?”

We’re supposed to finance our mission by broadcasting a reality show. I suddenly feel guilty for having unbroadcast emotions. “Isn’t this just the type of awkward interpersonal moment we should film to boost our ratings? Let’s flip on all the Channel Zilch cams so Dar doesn’t have to record every poignant moment. People will want to watch us while we orbit.”

Head calls piteously from middeck, “Makeup!”

Hel, “The reality show is postponed. We’ll kick off Channel Zilch when we’re in geosynchronous orbit.”

Darthy, “I’m recording a lot of good footage while we’re heading to geosynch.”

This news is nervous-making. “Fess up. Is the Zilch reality show ever going to broadcast? Please tell me we didn’t hotwire Enterprise for a celestial joyride with no re-entry plan.”

Darthy spins toward me and. “Stealing and launching Enterprise is an act that justifies itself. We’re in space! Besides, I’m recording a lot of good footage we’ll broadcast from geosynch when Channel Zilch goes live.”

Doesn’t quite make sense to me, but, “Just so you remember we need to earn cash to buy re-entry. Without Channel Zilch we’ve got no income. Time to start the show now!”

Hel’s eyes blaze as she asks, “Haven’t you listened to a thing we’ve said? Dar and I shouted it loud and clear before we launched: we stole Enterprise to kickstart the Singularity!”

“And that’ll make us rich how?”

Darthy, “We’ll kick off the show in geosynch. Richard and I didn’t come all this way to sit on our thumbs. Richard doesn’t run out of words and I’ll take pretty vids to keep our audience agog.”

I’m used to the feeling but still feel burned. “I’ve got a bad feeling the only reason I’m risking my ass is for your geek god.”

Heloise smiles one of her scarce, scary smiles.

Mama Energia, she is a mighty, mighty booster, but she’s not a magic chariot to Anywheresville, Outer Space. No way can she heave a huge honking space shuttle to our destination: geosynchronous orbit. We’re riding in a stable orbit running a little eccentric, 153 to 178 miles high, the usual LEO (Low Earth Orbit) 90-minute-around-the-world lap time.

Before we shift orbit, we call recess, gawking at Earth, gamboling in zero-g, floating and throwing things and generally acting like kids—the best use for space flight I can think of. Well worth the billions of taxpayer dollars invested in NASA and its (almost!) defunct space shuttle program. Thank you taxpayers one and all for subsidizing our weightless larking. Hope your kids can float around up here someday. I know they will.
