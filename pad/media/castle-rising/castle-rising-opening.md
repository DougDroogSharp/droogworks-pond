# Castle Rising
*YA historical science fiction · Doug Sharp*

---

## Chapter One

*Zbu: Earth Orbit. 2,102,122.11 Great Games Galaxy Time*

In the center of a vast ruby colored bubble floated a living model of Earth,half-shadowed with night, oceans glistening, tan continents splotched with living greens, white clouds swirling. Above the glinting ice fields of the South Pole hovered Zbu, which appeared to be a silver 3-bladed sword. They looked like a deadly tripod. Each blade was made from smaller swords made from smaller swords and so on until the smallest swords were only a few molecules long. This was the control room of Zbu’s spaceship, and they were ready to put their plan into action.

* * *

Zbu jangled, “Greetz! Are you ready to push these Humans to their limits?”

Each of the twenty-seven Greetz floating around the globe tooted excited green puffs of “Yessss!” from a glowing blue hole in the top of their head.

Greetz look like two soap bubbles stuck together. Their body is a transparent bubble about the size of an adult human. Their head is a pink bubble the size of a basketball. They can shrink themselves to microscope-tiny or swell as large as a sports stadium.

When Greetz need to see, dozens of tiny black eyebubbles sprout on their head.

When Greetz needs to speak, they toot colored vapor. Simple Greetz words are puffs of single colors. Sentences are knotted rainbows. Paragraphs are smoky tapestries.

Zbu clattered, “It is time to collect Earth’s treasures!”

“Yessss!” Greetz danced in excitement, spinning and weaving through a green cloud of agreement.

Zbu snipped, “Greetz, name your targets.”

As the Greetz puffed the names of their twenty-seven targets, intricate knots of colored vapor rose from their heads. As each target was named, a purple dot glowed on the globe.

“A Moai from Rapa Nui.” A purple dot on a tiny island in the biggest sea.

“Angkor Wat.” A dot near the coastof a giant continent.

“Sea Wolf, the Viking ship.” A dot in another sea.

Zbu listened patiently as the Greetz recited the list, ending with,

“Hopperstad Church.”

“Castle Rising.”

“Catacombs of Kom el Shoqafa.”

“Thuparamaya.”

“Hagia Sophia.”

Zbu jangled, “All are targeted accurately?”

The Greetz tooted greenly, “YESSS!”

Zbu clattered with excitement. “To your targets! FLY!”

A glowing blue dot appeared in the control room’s ruby wall. It widened into a ring of blue fire.

A breeze grew to a wind as air rushed out the glowing hole. The wind tossed and spun the Greetz. Leaking, howling air shoved the swarm of Greetz toward the hole.

As each flew through the blue ring, a tiny green light flared from its body bubble. Through the control room’s red wall, streaks of light scored the sky as Greetz zipped away to every target.

Zbu watched the miniature Earth to track the progress of the raid.

Twenty-seven threads of green light appeared above its surface. The track of each Greetz curved toward the Earth. As each green trail touched a purple dot, the dot turned red.

Zbu jangled with satisfaction, “Soon we will see what these Earthlings are made of.”

## Chapter Two

*Shenkasa: Great Zimbabwe*

Hill Complex, Great Zimbabwe Castle, Zimbabwe

* * *

Near, a leopard snarled. A Hyena coughed. Its pack answered with howling laughter.

It is time.

Shenkasa sat up from her bed, slowly, quietly. The room was dark. Sister Akasin snorted her high, whinnying snore. Her other sisters breathed steadily in sleep.

I will miss Akasin most.

I will not miss her snore.

It is time.

Time to leave family, friends, castle, land I love.

Time to start my journey to see all the world.

Shenkasa stood carefully and listened. No one woke. She walked slowly, silently to the door.

She turned and mouthed sadly, “Goodbye, sisters.”

She stepped into the castle courtyard lit by the Moon and flickering fire. The familiar tangy smell of burning Msasa wood.

The crackling blaze was tended by Auntie Tudziya, old and wise and kind. And blind.

Shenkasa touched the scar of her own recently-blinded eye, reminded again of the loss.

She looked at Auntie.

I shall miss her.

Careful. She will hear. No, the fire’s crackling will cover my steps.

She took a light step. Auntie’s head turned. Shenkasa felt the blind eyes upon her.

“Who walks?”

Answer her? No.

She took the lightest step as if stalking an Antelope.

“Who walks?” Auntie asked louder.

“I, Shenkasa, honored Aunt.”

“It is late.”

“The leopard woke me.”

“Sit with me.”

“I will do so… after I climb Great Bird Rock to thank the Moon.”

“You, child, climb Great Bird Rock? At this time of night?”

Aunt thinks I shouldn’t climb because of my… dislike of heights. Despite my fear, I will look upon Father’s land one last time.

Shenkasa answered politely, “The moon is bright.”

“May moonlight guide your feet safely.”

“I thank you, honored elder.”

Aunt Tudziya turned to stare into the fire with her blind eyes.

Shenkasa turned and stared into the night with her blind eye and her good eye.

She nodded to the sleepy guard as she left the royal enclosure where she lived with her royal sisters—daughters of the king’s first wife.

Great Bird Rock was a massive bird-shaped boulder, towering over the castle.  Only the royal family were allowed to climb its wooden steps. Her Father stood on top of it to conduct important ceremonies and to pass judgment on gold thieves and cattle rustlers.

Although she hated heights, Princess Shenkasa had climbed its steps many times, but never alone, and never at night.

But first she walked to the thicket of Acacia shrubs where she had hidden her supplies. She knelt and reached through their branches, ignoring the thorns which scratched at her arm. She pulled her spear from the bush.

Reaching again into the bushes, she grabbed a strap of her pack and dragged it free of the thorns.

I will wear this pack upon my back for many days.

To bless her pack and journey she reached into her pocket pouch and grasped her small black Great Bird carved from soapstone. She touched it to her forehead and thought a prayer.

Great Bird, fly above me.

Show me animals to hunt and enemies who would hunt me.

Show me the path I must travel to see all the world.

Great Bird, I ask you.

Great Bird, I thank you.

Tears stung her eye.

Must I leave my home?

There’s no future for me here. I’m a princess with one eye—a burden to my family. Father will never be able to marry me to a king.

Curse that leopard!

Pain in her eye had dulled. Pain in her heart would burn always.

Great Bird, you are cruel, but you are kind in equal measure.

I suffered your cruelty.

I ask you for an equal measure of kindness.

Shenkasa turned from her burning memory of leopard to her pack of trek supplies.

Check it one last time. Forgetting even one item might kill me. The trail to Sofala is hard.

She opened her pack and spread its contents on the ground.

Twenty days of dried meat. Two full gourds—water and milk. Two nuggets of gold. Leather sandals. A long strip of leather cord. Blanket to wrap myself against the cold nights. The precious iron knife given to me by Aunt Tudziya after my womanhood ceremony.

She turned again to the looming rock. Leaving her supplies on the ground, she  haltingly began to climb the narrow stairway that wound up the back of the rock, being careful not to look down. The steps were slippery with night damp. She breathed a deep breath of relief as she stepped onto the rock's broad top.

One last look at Father’s empire.

Standing on Great Bird Rock, she was silvered by moonlight.

Shenkasa was a girl of fifteen winters. She was tall, muscled with thin, hard cords, black as soapstone. Her face was proud, alert. Despite the scar where her right eye had been, she looked like a queen although she was but a princess.

The moon shone bright. Shenkasa saw for miles into the dark, hilly distance. She looked toward Sofala and the Great Water. The trail to Sofala she would travel tonight and tomorrow… many tomorrows.

The rising sun will hurt my eyes. My eye.

A flash of lightning lit the horizon.

No storm will stop me. I will go where I will, with no fear.

I will travel beyond Sofala to Egypt, to Jerusalem, to Rome. I will see all the world.

Shenkasa swallowed hard. She must now look down. She sat to lessen the trembling in her legs.

She lowered her eye to look at her home—Great Zimbabwe, her Father’s House of Stones. Its high rock walls loomed and curved with graceful power. The walls children were forbidden to climb. The walls she had climbed many times alone and with brothers and sisters.

She saw the cracked boulder where, eight winters in age, she had slipped and broken her arm. She smiled sadly.

No more forbidden childish games.

She shook off that thought and focused on the huts in the valley below. Her Father’s castle was a mighty castle.

This castle is all the world I’ve known. I shall miss its beauty, its strength, most of all the people who call it home.

She looked down for the last time at the walls of her great dwelling.

It is time.

Shenkasa forced herself to turn away.

Away from my home, from my people, from Akasin.

Resolutely, she stood again and began her descent. As she stepped onto the first wood step, she was startled by brightness from above. A fiery green streak flashed through the night sky. The streak speared the ground near Great Bird Rock.

“BOOM!”

The ground shook as if before a stampeding bull. Green light flared where the falling star hit.

Her foot slipped and she grabbed the railing. Her body twisted and she landed hard on slippery wood.

Panic gripped her body and mind. She clung to the railing.

I do not fear. I do not fear.

She looked at the spot where the star landed, and saw to her astonishment a clear bubble as big as her father, with a smaller pink bubble atop it. A pinpoint of harsh green light flared on the bottom of the thing. It bounced from the ground and floated toward the castle.

The bubble made no sound as it floated toward Great Zimbabwe Castle. It landed on the Castle wall and flashed blinding red. The thing melted into a fire veined skin which flowed over the wall and into the castle grounds.

Yells. Screams. Curses.

People ran. Bawling babies carried by screaming mothers. Aunt Tudziya hobbled hand in hand with Sister Akasin.

The demon skin pushes people, herding them like cattle.

I must join the people.

Shenkasa stood and began her descent. Once on the ground she grabbed her spear, shouldered her pack, and raced back to the castle.

## Chapter Three

*Magpie: Mesa Verde*

Far View Village, Mesa Verde, North America

* * *

In her room Magpie knelt in an angle of morning brightness. The sandstone wall behind her glowed like honey in the rising sun.

"Are you ready?" Her Mother’s stern teaching voice. Mother knelt in front of Magpie, precious deerskin bundle on her lap.

"I am ready, Beloved Mother."

She handed Mother her prayer stick. She mustn’t be proud of it, but every feather was chosen and placed true.

"It is new, Mother. Wet with dew, Mother. I made it this dawn."

Mother held it before her eyes, seeing, thinking, feeling.

She tried to hide a smile of pride. "Thank you for this thoughtful beauty."

Magpie blushed and looked away. "Thanks to birds who gave their beauty."

"To birds."

Her prayer stick was small in Mother’s hands. Mother held its cottonwood stick upright and rotated it slowly. She gently lifted each feather, turned it over, thumb testing its edge.

Magpie had chosen each feather with all her mind and heart.

East is yellow. I chose a yellow-headed blackbird feather.

Redwing blackbird red for North.

Then lazuli bunting blue for West.

Tanager orange for South.

Magpie black for Mother Night Sky—the direction Up.

Mother asked the joke she always asked, “Why magpie for black, not crow?”

She smiled as always. “I know a girl named Magpie.”

“Point to her.”

Magpie giggled and pointed to herself. This was their joke from the first time she had learned her Birth Color was black and her Path led to Mother Night.

I can’t tell Mother the deeper reason for using the magpie feather. It would make her sad.

Last night I dreamt of a magpie with Father’s voice singing Father’s song. The song that begins, "My little breath…"

Mother lifted the last feather.

White bald eagle head feather for Mother Earth Womb and the direction Down.

Mother sighed a sigh of satisfaction. Her daughter was her pride, only fifteen winters old, yet so wise. And still she grew in skill and strength. She would become a powerful shaman, like her mother.

Magpie’s mother grasped her precious deerskin bundle.

"Today I teach my daughter the power of the red-tailed hawk’s tail feather."

Mother carefully unrolled her deerskin bundle on the ground between them. As she unwound it the baby feathers appeared first, tiny and bundled in milkweed thread.

Magpie named them, "Red breast of robin. Precious warbler yellow. Orange of kinglet’s crown. The blackest magpie feathers are those from its chin."

I never tire of the beauty and power of the feathers.

Mother unrolled the last of the feathers. The final few were the longest: wing feather of a golden eagle, tail feather of a turkey.

I wish we still had our scarlet macaw blue tail feather—the most beautiful and powerful. But we traded it for food during the winter of hunger.

Magpie took a long, slow breath, remembering.

Later, Father journeyed far South to Chaco with a trading group, to exchange turquoise for a macaw feather, but none ever returned. I would trade every macaw in the world for Father!

"BOOM!"

A sound like a boulder falling from the cliff.

A frightened shout from outside. A scream. Screams. Panic.

Mother said, "Stay with the feathers!"

As Mother ran through the doorway Magpie heard a disgusting wet sound, like a pack of slobbering dogs licking thick corn mush.

What danger does Mother run to? I must help.

She rose on one knee.

The feathers. She told me to stay.

Her Mother’s commanding voice shouted to the crowd, loud but calm, "Stay back. Stay back!"

I must look. I’ll hold the feathers tight.

She rolled up the precious bundle, tied the cord, and hugged it, then heard a noise behind her.

Magpie turned and gasped.

The walls are wet! They’re breathing and flowing!

The wet skin flowed down the hallway on every surface. It slurped as it flowed.

No! It flows into Taiwa’s room!

Her best friend lived next door.

She shouted, "TAIWA! I AM HERE!"

No answer. Magpie ran to help her friend but halted at the doorway. The wet skin formed a barrier.

She put out her hand but jerked it back when the wet sound suddenly silenced.

Magpie called, "TAIWA!"

No answer.

She looked down and saw she was standing on a shiny crawling skin.

The skin jerked and pulled free from the floor. A ripping sound like the hide being torn from the flesh of a deer.

The walls caught fire. Red like a Redwing’s shoulder feathers in the sun.

Magpie clenched her eyes against the blaze and stepped backwards. Loose skin caught her heel. She stumbled and fell against the wall, clutching Mother’s precious bundle tight.

The radiant skin fell loose from the ceiling and walls. It shrank down the hallway. Through slitted eyelids she saw it move toward her.

Mother called, "Magpie! Come.”

She spun and ran toward the light of the doorway.

Magpie took one step outside. The People looked past her at their pueblo home.

She looked back. The pueblo’s tan bricks were coated with fiery skin.

Magpie blinked at the painful light and took another step back. She felt snow crunch, her foot slipped. She fell.

But her precious bundle was safe in her arms.

I must bring these feathers to Mother.

"MAGPIE!" Taiwa’s voice from the crowd.

"TAIWA!" she called.

Blessing! My friend is safe.

The flickering skin ripped from the pueblo and slowly inflated. As it swelled, the wall crept toward Magpie.

"Come to me, Magpie." Her Mother’s voice. Firm. Calm.

Magpie stood and stepped slowly back. She couldn’t look away from the powerful magic.

A vast hollow beat shook her. Giants’ drums on a cold winter night.

The bubble flashed purple Stellar Jay then went clear as air. It stopped swelling.

"Magpie!"

Magpie walked backwards toward Mother’s voice. As she retreated she saw…

Our pueblo is inside a gigantic bubble!

She stopped, overcome with awe.

## Chapter Four

*Will: Castle Rising*

Scriptorium, Castle Rising, England

Will slowly, precisely inked the thin black stripe and put down his brush.

Seven is the correct number of stripes for a leopard’s tail.

He sat up straight, stretched his cramped shoulders, rubbed his tired eyes.

Behind him through the stone doorway came a distant echoing clatter.

From the great grey halls of Castle Rising came sounds of alarm, and the rushing of armored soldiers.

Soldiers rarely walk the hallways this late—their racket wakes sleeping nobles.

The clank and creak of armor grew louder. Will grimaced at the painful noise. Jangle of chain mail, shouts, hurried metal footsteps.

A pang of fear.

No one must find me here. Father Simon will beat me.

Hide my leopard!

He carefully lifted the page, face up to keep dry its fresh ink.

He knelt under the table and slid his treasure up into its hiding place, a shelf where no one would think to look.

As the clatter echoed closer, Will sat on the hard bench, lay his head on the table, squeezed shut his eyes.

Remember this lie: I fell asleep grinding ink. Pray Father Simon won’t beat me for sleeping at my task.

As the soldier’s din grew louder Will gritted his teeth. His heart beat fast as he slowed his breathing. He waited, eyes shut, as the crash and rattle of armor echoed loud down the stone hallway, pounding his raw nerves.

The soldiers tromped up to the open doorway…

…and passed without pausing.

Will sagged with relief as the awful clanks faded. His secret was his secret still. His rump would not be beaten tonight.

A flea bit Will’s belly. He plucked it and flicked it away. “Begone, Sir Flea. Dine on Father Simon’s rump.”

Will walked to the stone fireplace and threw a log on the dying fire. He warmed his hands on the coals, though the room was toasty.

He sighed, closed his eyes, and let the holiness of the room soothe his soul.

This Scriptorium is where Jehovah has placed me to create wondrous books. I’m a scribe. Crafting books is my holy task in this sorrowful world.

Why does Father Simon stand in my way? I am young, but I am a far better illustrator than Brother Cuthbert or Brother John.

And yet instead of illuminating manuscripts, I am allowed only to ink letters,  or even worse scrape hair off stinking calf hides to make parchment.

Pray, St. Catherine, that Father Simon will let Will glorify Jehovah by painting his marvelous creation.

He opened his eyes. The Scriptorium blazed with the new log’s flaring light as if coated with gold leaf. Light illuminated hard benches and wooden tables. Here Will’s brother monks sat for hours each workday creating treasures with their brushes and quills.

The blaze lit Will’s short blond hair and cold-pink face. He was a stocky boy of fifteen Saint’s Days. On his cheeks and forehead were faint scars of chickenpox. He wore a brown woolen monk’s robe. His usual expression was one of puzzled innocence.

Will felt the warmth from the great hearth.

He breathed a deep breath of joyous pride.

This leopard is a fine first page for my bestiary. In my bestiary I will paint every animal.

He fetched his treasure from its secret place. He laid it on the slanted table and sat on the hard, cold bench to work. The hour was late but the noisy scare left Will alert and eager to create.

What could be better than a pot of fresh ink, a sharp quill, and a page of fresh parchment to illuminate?

Will cleared his mind’s slate and looked down at his leopard as if he had never seen it.

His leopard—a bold black outline filled with warm yellow ink made from egg white and precious saffron. The beast was banded with curved black stripes that rounded its solid shape.

Will’s leopard lunged ferociously across the page, roaring mouth baring cruel fangs, fierce claws reaching. The fire flared and the leopard’s eye glittered bright with hungry rage.

Will smiled at the beauty of his almost-living leopard.

My bestiary will show Father Simon and all the world what a magnificent book Will the Scribe can create.

But have I truly drawn a leopard or a striped and grumpy Cat? If only I could see a living leopard! Someday I must travel the world to see every animal so I can complete my bestiary.

I’ve painted my leopard with all my skill and must move on to my next animal. If I stare longer I will see every flaw and spend forever never quite making it perfect. A sinner striving for perfection is an insult to our perfect Lord.

Now to write 'Leopard' below the beast.

Will raised his quill to dip it into the pot of thick black ink. He heard a squelch behind him. Not soldiers but what?

Will turned his head and dropped his quill in astonishment.

The grey stone around the open doorway shone wetly.

It looks like it’s covered with living, shining skin!

The stuff crept steadily across the walls, floor, and ceiling of the Scriptorium.

I’m tired. I see things when I’m tired.

Will stood, mouth open, and watched the shiny stuff flowing across every stone, sounding like a sty of Pigs rolling in mud.

When the stuff approached Will’s feet he quickly hopped up on his bench and watched it wriggle under him, under the table until it coated the far wall. When he looked back toward the doorway all shone wetly.

The stuff of demons!

The room was completely covered in shiny, pulsing grease.

An overpowering sweet smell, like honey on a warm day.

Distant shouts and screams echoed from the doorway.

This demonic stuff must be everywhere in Castle Rising! The clattering knights ran from it!

I must pretend to be brave.

The wet sound stopped. The walls gave a sigh and the shine dulled.

Will looked down at the floor.

Dry? Dare I put my foot on it?

From where the demon skin covered the fireplace came a great wet “FSHLOWP!” Will nearly fell from the bench. The horrid noise sounded like skin ripped from a giant Ox.

Will covered his ears and prayed, “Silence, by the blessed wounds of Saint Catherine.”

The stuff hung loose over the fireplace. A net of glowing red veins throbbed on its surface.

Will’s eyes and mouth opened wide as he took in this awful wonder.

The glowing veined skin heaved toward Will. He gasped and stepped off the bench. The floor was spongy, but stable. The wobbling surface crackled with searing light as it moved toward him, peeling from the walls,  creeping closer. The skin’s edge glowed blue where it flowed around the legs of Will’s work table.

Will fought the urge to run.

I must get my leopard. I’ll stand and let the stuff flow around me like it did the table.

Will closed his eyes at the brightness. Hellfire flickered through his eyelids.

“AYEE!” Will yelled as the skin pushed him backwards. It was warm and soft like the belly of a cat and much stronger than a fifteen-year-old scribe.

The stuff shoved him toward the doorway. It ripped noisily from the walls, floor, and ceiling and pushed him into the hallway.

Screams, shouts, horrid horrid noise. A man ran past shrieking, “Satan! Satan!”

Screaming maids and knights and children ran from the nightmare stuff down the hallway toward the castle gatehouse.

My bestiary! I must leave it and flee with the crowd.

Will ran. The painful din of panicked screams and clattering armor battered Will’s ears. He fled with the crowd into the night. He leapt over a fallen, struggling knight.

In the distance he heard the Great Gate open—creaks and rattling chains.

Howling maids and bleating lords jostled as they ran past. A Pig squealed by.

Disgusting creature.

The crowd squeezed through the huge stone gatehouse. Will nearly fell when a sharp elbow jabbed his side. People were near panic as they jostled over the outer ditch’s bridge. Angry curses.

People spread out as they reached the land beyond the deep ditch. When the crowd stopped pushing, Will kept walking, trying to escape the noise and eyes.

A great tearing shriek silenced the crowd.

Will turned. Where Castle Rising should have been was a red-flickering skin inflating like a Pig-bladder ball.

If my eyes are bewitched may Saint Lucy strike me blind!

The castle-enclosing bubble swelled until it was a great half-globe. Its walls bulged outward, swallowing the gatehouse and then the ditch.

A terrible noise—a huge drum beaten slowly underwater. Will gritted his teeth at the pain.

Veins of pulsing fire flickered slower and dimmed on its surface. The bubble stopped growing. Its skin shivered, flashed a deep jewel-like purple, turned clear as holy water.

Castle Rising shone starkly moonlit through the clear skin.
