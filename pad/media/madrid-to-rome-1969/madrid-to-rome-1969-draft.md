# Madrid to Rome 1969
*Travel memoir · Doug Sharp · draft in progress*

---

My Dad was in the Air Force so we moved a lot. I went to 12 different schools before college. Our biggest moving adventure was in 1957 when we moved from Orlando to Fairbanks—a 5000 mile drive. The Alcan Highway wasn’t paved then, so we drove 1,200 of those miles on mud and gravel. I was 5 but that trip engraved strong memories.

In 66 we moved to the UK for 3 whole years. I was able to attend all 3 years of high school at London Central High School, an American school mainly for Air Force brats. Living in the UK in late 60s was a blast. I loved the UK and took full advantage of living there. I took the one-hour train ride to London at least once a month.

I loved to hitchhike. I took 3 trips around Cornwall and 3 trips around Wales. It was incredibly easy to get rides. Lots of small rides because I rarely travelled on major roads. I mostly stayed in youth hostels, but drivers often offered to let me stay in their homes.

In my junior year I started plotting an epic hitchhike--from Madrid to Rome. I’d start in Madrid because one of my best buddies, Mike Perren, lived there. We’d hitchhike together.

But first I took Mike on a test hitchhike. He hadn’t thumbed much so I wanted him to know what it was like.

So I took him hitchhiking around Wales. It was hilarious. Mike was a complainer and our trip brought out the worst in him. Food, weather, boredom all triggered his griping and grumbling. His bellyaching cheered me up immensely. I’m a naturally cheerful guy and his moaning was soooo dramatic. My laughter didn’t improve his mood.

(Welsh couple)

I was a castle fan so I’d try to visit all the castles along my routes. One of my favorite castle is Carreg Cennen. It’s perched picturesquely on a cliff and has a cool underground passage to a dungeon.

Summer 1969—time for my big adventure. Tahe family drove from the UK to Madrid.

Mike’s family was doing Madrid right. They learned Spanish and learned their way around Madrid. One night they took us to a tiny flamenco bar. We sat right by the stage. The flamenco performance boggled young Dougy. I’d never seen anything like the intense passion of the dance, dancer, and guitar. That memory is lodged firmly in my brain.

Mike had become very cool. He rolled up his sleeves in a way I started copying. Whenever I roll up my sleeves I think of Mike. He also seemed to know about girls.
